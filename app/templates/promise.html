Safety & privacy

{{ d.paw(22) }}
AI reads, rules decide

The AI never sets urgency or picks who goes. Unit-tested code does, the same way every time.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
The AI never invents first aid

Safety tips come only from reviewed cards based on People For Animals, Humane World for Animals, Blue Cross and WHO, with sources shown.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Private by default

No phone numbers are shared. Public maps and posters show an area, never an exact spot. Photos are re-encoded, which strips hidden GPS data.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
For animals, never against them

There is no “remove this dog” button. StrayWatch follows the Animal Birth Control Rules, 2023: care, vaccinate, sterilise.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Humans confirm, AI assists

When it's unsure who an animal is, it asks. It acts on its own only at 95%+ confidence.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Responders are protected too

Volunteers get alerts only within 8 km, at most 6 a day, and can type “pause” any time. Photos with no animal alert nobody.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Every decision is on record

Each case keeps a ledger of what every agent and person did. Reporters see their own case's steps through a private link.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Consent under DPDP 2023

We keep only what a rescue needs, and partner details only to follow up. Ask us to delete your data any time.

{{ d.paw(22) }}
Not an emergency service for people

StrayWatch helps people help animals. If a person is hurt, call 112.
