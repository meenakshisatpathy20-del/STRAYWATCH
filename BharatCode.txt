The problem
-----------
Every day on Indian roads, stray and community animals are hit, injured or fall sick, and help reaches them late.
Rescuers, vets and city teams exist, but they find out too late, without a photo or an exact location, and nobody
owns the case. An injured animal lying in a lane also becomes the next road accident.

What it does: one journey, three parts
--------------------------------------
StrayWatch is a free, installable web app (with an optional WhatsApp add-on) in English, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil,
Telugu and Marathi. It follows an animal from the moment it is hurt to the day it is healthy again.

1. Rescue (the core): anyone sends a photo, a few words or a landmark. The AI reads it, tested rules set the urgency,
   and the right person nearby is alerted: the animal's caretaker first, then a rescue NGO, a vet, the municipal
   cattle team or traffic police. If nobody answers in 3 minutes it moves on by itself. A case closes only with a
   photo proof the AI checks; the reporter tracks every step. Rescuers get a private chat with the reporter,
   "open cases near me", transport requests and nearby animal hospitals; new helpers are approved by a coordinator.
2. Treatment: an AI pre-check writes a one-page summary for a verified vet, who joins a live chat (each side in their
   own language) or a booked video / clinic slot. Emergencies go to the 1962 animal ambulance first. Vaccination
   schedules, a food guide and pay-on-delivery food (including a street-dog feeding pack) support recovery.
3. Fewer accidents: driver warnings while an injured animal is on the road, a hotspot map of where and when animals
   get hurt, and Lost & Found that matches lost animals against every photo sent nearby.

Paid pet consults, food orders and city dashboards are planned to keep rescue free for citizens and rescuers.

How the BharatCode model API is used
------------------------------------
All model calls go through the BharatCode (Adaption) Responses API with the official `adaption` Python SDK
(app/ai.py). Hosted models:
- qwen3.5-397b (vision): reading a report photo + Hinglish / Indian-language message into a validated triage record
  (~2-3 s); recognising a registered animal among look-alikes (~1.2 s); the vet pre-check summary; matching lost
  animals; batch translation of every reply into the person's language.
- gemma-4-31b-it (vision): checking the rescuer's proof photo; comparing a check-in photo with the last one;
  understanding chat intent when keywords don't match.
Design rule: the model reads and compares; plain, unit-tested code decides and enforces (urgency, who is alerted,
helper radius, escalation timers, privacy, confidence thresholds). Every reply is validated field by field with a
safe fallback, and every call is timed and shown in a live AI trace on the demo page.

Proof
-----
101 automated tests in GitHub Actions; a two-city load test of 1,238 reports (every case got a responder, 0 alerts
beyond 8 km or to the wrong city). Honest limits: responders and vets on the live site are labelled samples until
real partners join; care cards await review by a partner vet.
